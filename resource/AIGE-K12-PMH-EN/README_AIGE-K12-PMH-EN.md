## 2. Background: AIGE in Practice

Artificial intelligence is increasingly involved in such foundational social activities as knowledge production, information dissemination, judgment formation, and social action. Students therefore need to develop a progressive understanding of the basic concepts, methods, applications, risks, ethics, and social impacts of AI, as well as the ways of thinking, judgment, and sense of responsibility required in an intelligent society.

The Tsinghua Artificial Intelligence General Education system (AIGE) is a systematic educational initiative developed in response to this transformation. It connects basic education, higher education, teacher education, and lifelong learning. The system is jointly developed by Tsinghua University’s Department of Computer Science and Technology, Tsinghua University Press, Tsinghua University High School, Tsinghua University Primary School, and partner organizations.

AIGE employs a collaborative approach to curriculum development. Universities and AI scholars provide the academic foundation and design the knowledge structure; education experts develop the curriculum; and frontline teachers continuously evaluate and refine the teaching content through classroom practice. These efforts have produced an integrated system connecting textbooks, courses, presentation slides, student handbooks, teaching plans, teacher training, and classroom pilots. AIGE has been implemented in more than 300 pilot schools and has established a teaching network involving more than 200 certified instructors.

## 3. Objectives

These English-language materials are intended to:

1. Help schools establish a coherent and progressive K–12 AI general education curriculum;
2. Provide direct support for lesson preparation, classroom instruction, discussion, and student activities;
3. Lower the resource barrier for schools and nonprofit educational organizations seeking to launch AI general education courses;
4. Support teacher training, curriculum pilots, and educational research;
5. Provide a foundation for curriculum translation, localization, and adaptation in different countries and regions; and
6. Help students understand the foundational knowledge of the intelligent era and develop critical judgment, ethical awareness, a sense of responsibility, and cognitive autonomy.

## 5. Progression from Primary to High School

The three textbooks and their companion resources follow a progressive structure: **stories and experiences – knowledge networks – in-depth exploration**.

### Primary School: Beginning with Stories and Experiences

At the primary-school level, students encounter artificial intelligence through stories, images, notable figures, historical events, familiar intelligent applications, and classroom activities. The emphasis is on stimulating interest, building an initial understanding, and developing basic awareness of safety, honesty, and responsibility.

### Middle School: Building a Knowledge Network

At the middle-school level, students organize their prior experiences into an interconnected knowledge structure. They begin to develop a systematic understanding of foundational AI concepts, typical tasks, application scenarios, technical methods, and social issues. They also gradually learn how data, models, training, outputs, and evaluation relate to one another.

### High School: Engaging in In-Depth Exploration

At the high-school level, students explore AI principles, frontier technologies, complex systems, human–AI collaboration, ethical risks, social impacts, and future developments in greater depth. They are expected to analyze more complex cases, understand the boundaries of technological capabilities, and make informed judgments about choices, risks, and responsibilities in AI applications.

## 6. Two-Level Design of the Primary-School PPT Slides: L1 and L2

Each primary-school lesson provides two levels of PPT slides:

* **L1: Basic Classroom Version.** This version uses more images and less text and is suitable for direct classroom instruction, discussion, and student activities.
* **L2: Extended Learning Version.** This version contains more text and background knowledge and is suitable for teacher self-study, lesson preparation, curriculum adaptation, localization, and teacher training.

This two-level design keeps the classroom materials accessible and age-appropriate for younger students while providing teachers with richer background knowledge and greater scope for curriculum extension. Teachers may use L1, L2, or a combination of the two according to students’ ages, available class time, and local curriculum requirements.

## 7. Key Features

### 1. Complete K–12 Coverage

The resources span primary, middle, and high school, forming a continuous and progressive curriculum structure that supports long-term curriculum planning.

### 2. Alignment with a Complete Textbook Series

The PPT slides, teaching plans, and student handouts are aligned with the Tsinghua AIGE Series, supporting the transition from textbook content to classroom implementation.

### 3. Designed for Real Classrooms

The resources include instructional content, discussion questions, student activities, and implementation recommendations, directly supporting teachers in lesson preparation and classroom organization.

### 4. Support for Both Student Learning and Teacher Development

The classroom materials are clear, intuitive, and age-appropriate, while the extended materials give teachers opportunities to deepen their understanding of both AI knowledge and educational design.

The English-language materials provide a foundation for international schools, educators outside China, educational organizations, and localization teams. ## 8. Recommended Use

First-time users are advised to follow these steps:

1. Read this README and the release and usage information in the relevant Zenodo record;
2. Select the appropriate PPT package for primary, middle, or high school;
3. Consult the corresponding textbook, teaching plans, and student handouts;
4. Select teaching content according to class time, students’ ages, and prior knowledge;
5. Verify the local suitability of the examples, tools, platforms, and third-party materials;
6. Complete any necessary adaptations for language, culture, and curriculum standards;
7. Conduct a complete teacher-led trial run or rehearsal before formal classroom use; and
8. Record classroom feedback and submit suggestions through GitHub Discussions or Issues.
